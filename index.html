Methods

Two counting rules

A promise only counts as delivered on time when a filing proves the thing promised actually happened by then. Two rules make that precise.

  1. 01

    Starting isn't finishing

    If a filing promised to begin work by a date, starting by that date counts as on time. If it promised to finish or switch on, breaking ground doesn't count. Only completion or energization does.

    Example (hypothetical): "Energize by March 31." A later filing says construction began in February and power came on May 12. On time? No. Counted as delivered May 12.

  2. 02

    The calendar doesn't get credit

    Monthly reports cover a whole month, so the month's last day proves nothing on its own. We use the first date a filing actually proves delivery: the exact date if it gives one, otherwise the date the report was filed.

    Example (hypothetical): "Online by June 30." A June operating update, filed July 8, reports the site live with no exact date. Counted as delivered July 8, not June 30.
