What a Delivery Date Delivers

Originally published Nov 11, 2026 on Substack.

Three AI data-center builders reported between November 4 and November 9. Each gave dates. Each date marked a different moment.

On November 4, Riot Platforms put a schedule in its third-quarter release that reads like a timetable. Its Corsicana campus is leased in full to a tenant the release calls “a leading Frontier AI Lab”. The delivery schedule is “400 MW in September 2027, 200 MW in January 2028 and 310 MW in July 2028, with rent on each phase commencing no later than 60 days after delivery of its powered pads.”

Read the last clause twice. The date is not when computers switch on. It is when Riot hands the tenant ground with power on it. The release spells out the division of labor: Riot “delivers the land, the power infrastructure, and the powered pads, while continuing to own and maintain the high- and medium-voltage infrastructure that will serve them.”

That is one meaning of delivery. The other two releases offered more.

The first hall, not the campus

Hut 8 reported the same day. Its dates are counted in data halls: “initial data hall delivery targeted for Q2 2027 at River Bend, Q3 2027 at Beacon Point Phase 1, and Q2 2028 at Beacon Point Phase 2.”

The word “initial” carries weight. River Bend has four data halls. The release says the quarter’s work included “completion of structural steel and building foundations across all four data halls and initial energization of the campus substation.” The target is for the first hall.

Two of those hall quarters were on record before. In August, Hut 8’s second-quarter release said River Bend was “targeted for initial data hall delivery in the second quarter of 2027”, and that Beacon Point Phase 1 was targeted for “initial data hall delivery in the third quarter of 2027.” Three months later both quarters are unchanged, and the company writes that construction “remains on track to meet our delivery targets”. The Beacon Point Phase 2 date, the second quarter of 2028, first appears in the November release.

Its chief executive, Asher Genoot, described the date as something owed. “We view each project we undertake as an obligation to the tenants, lenders, communities, and shareholders to whom we have committed”, he said. “The first of those obligations comes due with the delivery of the first data hall at River Bend in the second quarter of 2027”.

One line from August does not reappear in the November release. Then, Beacon Point was “targeted for initial energization in the first quarter of 2027”. The new release describes construction of the campus substation but does not restate that quarter. A line left out of a press release is not a changed date, and we do not read it as one. It is simply the next date to check.

Dates with conditions printed beside them

HIVE Digital Technologies reported on November 9 and wrote its conditions next to its dates. The company “expects its energized footprint to reach approximately 540 MW following the planned energization of an additional 100 MW power purchase agreement at Yguazú in the fourth quarter of calendar 2026.”

Its near-term cloud target is stated “subject to financing, market conditions, deployment schedules and customer acceptance.” Of a longer-range goal, the release is blunter still: “This is a forward-looking target, not contracted revenue, and depends on financing, construction, customer commitments, commissioning, and market conditions.”

Then there is the hardware. “We anticipate these GPUs will ship before calendar year-end, after which the lender’s funds will be released from escrow to the GPU OEM as milestones are met.” A shipping date is a delivery date too, owed by someone else.

Reading the calendar

Set the three releases side by side and the word “delivery” names at least four different moments: powered pads handed to a tenant, the first hall of a multi-hall campus, a block of power switched on, and a shipment of chips leaving a vendor. Each is a real milestone. None of them, by itself, is the day a customer’s work starts running.

The companies’ own legal language lists the steps. Hut 8’s forward-looking statements cover “the development, construction, energization, commissioning and delivery of River Bend and Beacon Point”. That is five stages in one phrase. Riot’s names the risks between them: “construction plans, delays, supply chain issues, permitting or regulatory hurdles, and unforeseen technical challenges”.

This is why we keep the exact words. A target for the first hall is a different promise from a target for the campus. A date tied to powered pads is a different promise from a date tied to running servers. When one of them moves, the move is information, not wrongdoing. The next quarter’s filings will show which of these dates held.
