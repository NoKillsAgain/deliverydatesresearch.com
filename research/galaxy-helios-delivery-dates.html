The Date That Held

Originally published Nov 13, 2026 on Substack.

Galaxy Digital set a June 30 deadline for the first phase of its Helios campus. Its later filings say the date held. Two more dates are now on the page.

For three quarterly and annual reports in a row, Galaxy Digital described the first phase of its Helios campus in the West Texas panhandle with the same deadline. Its quarterly report of November 10, 2025 put it this way: “The retrofit of Helios will be completed in phases, with the full 133 MW of initial critical IT load expected to be delivered by the end of the first half of 2026.” The annual report filed February 26 repeated it.

The first-quarter release of April 28 and the quarterly report filed May 8 changed the wording. “The retrofit of Helios will be completed in phases, with substantially all of the 133 MW of critical IT load expected to be delivered by the end of the first half of 2026.”

“Full” became “substantially all.” The deadline stayed at June 30.

What the later filings say

Galaxy reported the second quarter on August 5. Its results release, filed as an exhibit to an 8-K, says: “Phase I was delivered on schedule, with rent commencement under the Phase I lease scaling with delivered capacity throughout the second quarter of 2026.” It also gives the count at the deadline: “all 133 MW of critical IT load under the Phase I lease in service by quarter end.”

The quarterly report filed August 10 switched to the past tense. The campus “has developed the first 133 megawatts” of critical IT load for CoreWeave, its tenant. The third-quarter release, filed October 27, repeats it: “with all 133 MW of critical IT load delivered to CoreWeave by the end of the second quarter.”

One step inside Phase I ran late. Galaxy’s fourth-quarter release, filed February 3, said the first data hall was “expected to be delivered in Q1.” The April 28 release says: “Delivery of first data hall occurred in April 2026.”

In May the wording was “substantially all.” In August the company reported all 133 megawatts. By Galaxy’s own filings, the Phase I deadline for all 133 megawatts was delivered on time.

Phase II: from a year to a quarter

The next date belongs to the larger phases. The February annual report said the next 393 megawatts of critical IT load, for Phases II and III, “is expected to be delivered starting in 2027.” The May report repeated it.

In April the year narrowed to a half. The first-quarter results release, filed April 28, says: “Phase II data hall deliveries are expected to commence in the first half of 2027.” In July, a presentation filed with an 8-K for a Phase II notes offering listed “Q2’27” as the “Initial Targeted Rent Commencement Date.” In August the delivery wording narrowed to the quarter too. Both the August and November quarterly reports say the capacity is “expected to be delivered starting in the second quarter of 2027.”

The October release adds a status line: “Helios I, Phase II is fully financed and under construction, with both buildings and the supporting substation progressing on schedule and within budget, and with construction activity expected to ramp significantly over the coming months, with data hall deliveries scheduled to begin in the second quarter of 2027.” The quarterly update presentation filed with it lists Phase II at 260 megawatts, with initial rent commencement in the second quarter of 2027, and Phase III at 133 megawatts in 2028.

Going from “2027” to “the first half of 2027” to “the second quarter of 2027” narrows the window. It is not a move, and we do not count it as one. The word that matters is “starting.” The date is for the first data halls, not the last.

A checkpoint the filings now carry

The August and November quarterly reports also carry a risk factor that the February annual report did not. “Specifically, in August 2026, the Governor of Texas directed the Public Utility Commission of Texas (“PUCT”) and ERCOT to conduct a comprehensive verification and audit of all data centers advancing through ERCOT’s interconnection process, and PUCT and ERCOT must complete this audit before any data center project moves forward.” Then: “This review may impact the power approval timelines for certain of our data center sites.”

The power for Phases II and III is already approved, according to the filing. “In total, the Electric Reliability Council of Texas (“ERCOT”) has approved over 1.6 gigawatts (“GW”) of gross power capacity at our Helios campus.” The filing says 600 megawatts of that total will support the Phase II and III leases. The next request is not approved yet: “At our Helios campus, we have submitted a 1 GW load request, which remains subject to ERCOT’s interconnection process and approval, and have identified the potential for an additional 1 GW of expansion.”

The filing does not link the audit to the Phase II date, and neither do we.

What we will check

Under our rule, a window is due at its end. Phase II’s first data halls are due by June 30, 2027. Phase III, listed for 2028, is due by December 31, 2028. We will read each quarterly report against those dates, and against the two sentences above about the audit and the load request.

Dates of this kind do not always hold. Of the 79 past-due promises in our ledger, 29 slipped (36.7%): 21 were delivered late and 8 were moved later. When a date moves, that is information, not wrongdoing. At Helios, the Phase I deadline held, though the first data hall came in April, after its first-quarter target.
