We corrected our own numbers. Here is exactly what changed.

Originally published Oct 26, 2026 on Substack.

Update, 2026-10-29: We re-ran every row under one rule: a promise counts as delivered on the earliest date the company’s filings allow. The current figures are 29 of 79 past-due promises slipped (36.7%). The median late delivery was 62 days late, with a range of 1 to 366 days. These replace the figures below.

We hold our numbers to the standard we hold the companies to: what was said, what the filings show, and the date it changed. Today that standard applied to us.

On October 26 we re-checked ten promises we had recorded as having no later word, against later SEC filings. Five now count as slipped, three as delivered on time, and two stay as no later word.

Updated figures: 27 of 79 past-due promises slipped (34.2%). Among those delivered late, the median was at least 63 days late, with a range of 1 to 459 days. These replace the figures in the earlier edition.

The re-check cut both ways. Two of the ten, in each company's own words:

Keel Infrastructure (formerly Bitfarms), Washington State, 6 MW. Promised: "the next 6 MW of in-process applications are expected to be energized in the first half of 2023" (40-F, filed 2023-03-21, EDGAR filing). Later filing: "In February 2024, the power supplier established the electricity connection allowing the Company, subject to the completion of the civil work, to access 6 MW of hydro power capacity." (40-F, filed 2024-03-07, EDGAR filing). We had it as no later word. It is now delivered late, 216 days. (An earlier edition counted to October 2024, when the company said its new 6 MW data center was fully constructed, and gave 459 days.)

Bitdeer, Tydal, Norway, Phase 1. Promised for April 2025 (20-F, filed 2025-04-21, EDGAR filing). Later filing: "Tydal, Norway: 70 MW of available power capacity was energized in April 2025." (6-K, filed 2025-05-09, EDGAR filing). We had it as no later word. It is now delivered on time.

The same rule moved one company's record up and another's down. That is the point: the filings decide, not us.
