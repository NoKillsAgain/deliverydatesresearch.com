September became “Q4 to Q1”: how Cipher’s Barber Lake date moved across five filings

Originally published Nov 4, 2026 on Substack.

One date in September 2025. A phased window running into 2027 by November 2026. Here is the paper trail, filing by filing.

Hi. We read the next filing so you don’t have to.

Cipher was the first of the companies we follow to report this earnings week. Its Q3 10-Q landed Monday, Nov 2. The sentence we cared most about wasn’t in the headline numbers. It was the one about when Barber Lake gets handed to its tenant.

Here’s how that sentence changed over three filings.

September 2025: the promise

When Cipher signed the Fluidstack lease, its 8-K filed Sep 25, 2025 said: “The Company expects to complete the development and construction of the Facility and deliver the Facility to Fluidstack by September 2026.”

August: one month

In the Q2 10-Q, filed Aug 4, 2026, Cipher described Barber Lake like this:

“The Company is in the process of building the Barber Lake Facility for Fluidstack USA II Inc. (“Fluidstack”), with an expected commencement date of September 2026.”

One facility, one tenant, one month. Clean and easy to check.

September: the bridge

On Sep 25, 2026, Cipher filed an 8-K about an amendment to the Fluidstack lease. That’s where the single month turned into a window:

“In connection with the Amendment, the Company and Fluidstack agreed to certain tenant-modification change orders to modify the design of the Barber Lake Facility as well as to an updated phased delivery schedule for the Barber Lake Facility, with individual data halls now expected to be delivered between the fourth quarter of 2026 and the first quarter of 2027.”

The same filing gives the first rent date:

“Rent will commence for each data hall as it is delivered, with the first rent commencement expected to occur during the fourth quarter of 2026.”

Two things to notice. First, the filing ties the new schedule to design change orders agreed with the tenant. Second, “delivered” now applies hall by hall, not to the whole facility at once.

November: the 10-Q confirms it

Monday’s Q3 10-Q carries the same window into the quarterly report:

“In September 2026, the Company amended the Fluidstack Lease to provide for an updated phased delivery schedule, with individual data halls expected to be delivered between the fourth quarter of 2026 and the first quarter of 2027 and remove the two five-year extension options.”

The extension options weren’t simply dropped: Cipher’s 8-K filed Nov 2, 2026 (accession 0001819989-26-000047, Exhibit 99.1) says it “Expanded the Barber Lake lease to a 20-year term,” through an amendment that “adds a second 10-year term directly with a leading AI lab.”

So, side by side:

Filing: What it said about Barber Lake timing

8-K, filed Sep 25, 2025: “deliver the Facility to Fluidstack by September 2026”

10-Q, filed Aug 4, 2026: “expected commencement date of September 2026”

8-K, filed Sep 25, 2026: halls “now expected to be delivered between the fourth quarter of 2026 and the first quarter of 2027”

10-Q, filed Nov 2, 2026: halls “expected to be delivered between the fourth quarter of 2026 and the first quarter of 2027”

One date became a phased window. That’s a change in the company’s own words, and it was disclosed, in writing, in the next filing. That’s exactly the kind of thing we track. It isn’t a judgment on anyone; schedules move, and the filings told us when this one did.

The Texas backdrop

There’s one more paragraph in Monday’s 10-Q worth reading if you follow Texas builds. It covers ERCOT’s review of large data-center loads:

“ERCOT currently expects to issue the related reports by December 10, 2026. In the meantime, ERCOT has temporarily paused certain energization approvals for large data center loads pending completion of that process.”

To be clear, the 10-Q doesn’t link the Barber Lake schedule change to this pause, and we won’t either. The amendment came in September, and the filing’s stated reason is the tenant change orders. But Dec 10 is now a public date in Cipher’s own filing, and it lands in the middle of the new delivery window. That makes it one to put on the calendar.

How we log it

In our records, this is promise CIFR-001, Barber Lake delivered to the customer, originally due by Sept 30, 2026. We log it as moved later: the company set a new, later window in a filing before the original date passed.

It’s one row in a bigger picture. We currently track 79 dated promises whose due dates have passed. 29 of them slipped (36.7%): 21 were delivered late and 8 were moved later. For the late ones, the median was 62 days late, and the range runs from 1 day to 366.
