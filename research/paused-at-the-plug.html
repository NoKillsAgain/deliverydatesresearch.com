Paused at the plug

Originally published Nov 4, 2026 on Substack.

Texas’s December 10 date now sits under every Texas delivery promise

A data center can be built, wired and ready, and still wait on one approval it does not control: permission to turn on.

In Texas, that approval is on pause.

We are currently tracking delivery dates for eight companies building data centers and mining sites. This week, one date that none of them set started to matter for the ones building in Texas: December 10, 2026.

What ERCOT said

ERCOT is the Texas grid operator. In a Batch Zero update for its September 14–15 board meeting, it listed the actions it took after the governor's August 3 directive:

"Paused approvals to energize data centers or virtual currency mining facilities (crypto facilities) that are 75 MW or greater."

And when the pause is meant to end:

"ERCOT has paused the energization of new Large Load data centers and crypto mining facilities until the completion of the verification and audit and community impact review."

Two reports close that review. ERCOT gave both the same deadline:

"ERCOT will file the Batch Zero Eligibility Verification Report with the Commission by December 10, 2026."

"ERCOT will file the Community Impact Review Report with the Commission by December 10, 2026."

So the date is not a delivery date. It is a filing date for a grid operator. But for a large new load in Texas, it is now the gate in front of the switch.

The news hook

On November 4, the Texas Tribune reported that the pause could run longer after Tuesday's governor's race:

"Data center approvals could remain paused in Texas until legislators pass new state laws next year if Democrat Gina Hinojosa keeps her campaign promises after winning the governor's race in a stunning victory Tuesday."

The same piece notes the original timeline:

"The audit by grid regulators and operators was expected to last until December."

We don't have a view on the politics. We read filings. The useful question for us is narrower: which companies have told investors about this, in their own words?

Who has named it

Cipher Digital filed its Q3 10-Q on November 2. It names the pause and the date directly:

"ERCOT currently expects to issue the related reports by December 10, 2026."

"In the meantime, ERCOT has temporarily paused certain energization approvals for large data center loads pending completion of that process."

Cipher also says where it stands in line:

"We have completed submissions in connection with ERCOT's initial Batch Zero eligibility verification and state and community impact review processes."

Note what that sentence says, and what it doesn't. Cipher's submissions are complete. ERCOT's review is not. The next step belongs to the grid operator.

IREN has not filed its new quarter yet. Its most recent annual report, the 10-K filed August 27, already flagged the risk before the pause had a December date attached:

"Due to recent changes in ERCOT's Batch Zero procedures, there may be delays in the energization of, or changes to the energization levels at, projects in Texas, including the Company's new and existing projects."

That is an August filing. We are not reading anything into IREN's next one until it exists.

Soluna hasn't named the pause in the filings we checked, but its September 8-Ks show the other side of Batch Zero, a classification:

"...ERCOT has conditionally classified its 100 MW Project Dorothy campus as Base Load in Batch Zero."

"Conditionally" is the word to hold onto. ERCOT's own update says final inclusion will be decided from the results of the eligibility verification, "provided in December."

Applied Digital, Bitdeer, HIVE, Keel and TeraWulf: in the SEC filings and attached press releases we searched since August 1, we found no sentence naming the ERCOT pause. That is a statement about the text we read, not about their projects.

What a delivery date means now

A delivery date in a filing is a company's promise about its own work: land, power gear, buildings, tenants.

Energization in Texas, for a new load of 75 MW or more, now sits behind a step outside any of that. A company can finish everything it controls and still be waiting on a report due December 10, and on whatever comes after it.

That changes how we read a Texas date. Going forward, when a covered company gives a Texas delivery date, we will log next to it whether the filing mentions the ERCOT pause, and whether the date was set before or after August 3.

For context, across the promises we already track, 29 of 79 have slipped (21 late, 8 moved), or 36.7%, with a median slip of 62 days and a range of 1 to 366 days. Those numbers don't account for this pause. December 10 is the next date we will check the Texas ones against.

What we'll watch

  • December 10: whether ERCOT files both reports on time.

  • After that: whether the energization pause lifts, and what the filings say when it does.

  • IREN: its new quarter, read against its own dates, once it is filed.

We'll report what each filing says when it lands.
