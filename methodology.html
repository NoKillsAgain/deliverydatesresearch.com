Methodology

How we score a delivery promise

Every row starts with a date a company promised in an SEC filing, then a later filing that says what happened. We log both quotes.

1. Find the promise. A dated commitment in an 8-K, 6-K, 10-Q, 10-K or similar EDGAR filing — facility energize, construction complete, commence operations, ready-for-service, and the like.

2. Find the follow-up. A later filing that states delivery, a moved date, or that the company is still silent past the due date.

3. Label the outcome. A past-due promise slips when it was delivered late or the date was moved later. On the current lock, 29 of 79 slipped (36.7%) = 21 delivered late + 8 moved to a later date. Among late deliveries the median was 62 days late; the range is 1 to 366 days.

Counting rules (Rule A). Days late run from the last day of the promised window (a promise for "July 2023" is due July 31, 2023) to the earliest date the later filing allows ("energized in October" counts from October 1). Moved later means a later filing pushed the date out before delivery; moved rows carry no day count, so the median and range cover the 21 late deliveries only. A begin-work promise counts as on time only if the filing promised a start by that date. For monthly report periods, we use the first date a filing proves delivery, not the last day of the month.

What we do not do. No price targets, no dollar amounts in public copy, no model output as a finding. The row is the filing text.
